================================================================================
REPLAY BUFFER SIZE: THE LARGEST SINGLE LEVER IN THIS CAMPAIGN
+15.17 INT8 points, and the seed variance that limited every other conclusion
collapses 6x.
================================================================================

DESIGN
Three buffer sizes x 3 seeds, all resuming from the validated smallc64_s5,
early stopping disabled, 25,000 episodes. At ~40 steps/episode a run collects
~1.0M transitions, so the arms span the full meaningful range:
    200k -> retains  20%   (the setting used for this entire project)
    500k -> retains  50%
      1M -> retains 100%   (no eviction at all)
Anything above 1M is bit-identical to 1M at this run length. The arms are
identical until episode 5,041, when the 200k buffer begins evicting -- and
that is exactly where the curves separate.

TRAINING (grasp success, final 5000 episodes)
    200k   90.4 89.3 88.8   mean 89.50
    500k   92.5 92.2 91.2   mean 91.94   +2.44  t=9.58 p=0.011  3/3 up
      1M   92.8 93.3 92.9   mean 92.97   +3.47  t=6.14 p=0.026  3/3 up
The 200k arm STOPS IMPROVING after block 3 (88.9 -> 88.9 across 18,000
episodes) while 1M is still climbing at the end (92.7 -> 94.4).

END-TO-END (12 eval seeds x 100, A+C rule layer)
  buffer   INT8 per-seed              mean     sd
    200k   91.50  57.33  77.58       75.47   17.18
    500k   86.33  95.17  90.42       90.64    4.42

THE VARIANCE RESULT IS THE IMPORTANT ONE
INT8 sd falls 17.18 -> 4.42 -> 2.84, monotonically in buffer size. The 200k arm
contains a catastrophic seed (57.33% INT8, 70.42% FP32); neither larger arm
produces one. Every "configuration X does not help" verdict in this campaign
was limited by exactly this spread -- INT8 outcomes from 51.50% to 93.83%
across nominally identical runs. Buffer size is the first variable measured
that reduces it. 1M has the lower mean but the better pair split (32/3 vs
24/12) and the lowest variance; 500k vs 1M is within noise on the mean.

A CONFOUND THAT MUST BE TESTED BEFORE ATTRIBUTING THIS TO TRAINING
The gain is INT8-specific: FP32 does not move (both Wilcoxon ns, and 500k is
15/21 DOWN on pairs). qat_and_convert.py calibrates the INT8 model on the run's
own replay_buffer.npz, so a 1M buffer supplies 5x more diverse calibration
states than a 200k one. Part of this may be better CALIBRATION rather than a
better policy. Directly testable by converting a 200k-trained actor using a
1M run's calibration set; that test is queued.

A CORRECTION TO EARLIER REASONING IN THIS CAMPAIGN
Before launching, this hypothesis was downgraded on the argument that the
project already retains a larger FRACTION than v7 did (200k/702k = 28.5%
against v7's 4M/28M = 14.3%). That reasoning was wrong. Retention fraction is
not the operative quantity; what matters is the absolute diversity held in the
buffer and how fast old experience is evicted relative to policy drift. The
200k arm stopped learning entirely while 1M kept improving.
