================================================================================
WHY TRANSPORT STALLS -- a measured decomposition of the end-to-end loss
Three causes, roughly equal. None of them is the transport policy.
================================================================================

MOTIVATION
Sec 9.x reports FP32 89.58% / INT8 76.08% at random spawn (1200 episodes each),
with the grasp stage reaching TRANSPORT in 1200/1200 and every remaining
failure downstream. "Downstream" was as far as the analysis went: all failures
carry the same DONE_FAIL label, so the 10.4 / 23.9 lost points were a single
undifferentiated bucket, and the rule-layer sweep could not act on them.

stall_diag.py instruments the carry itself -- distance to bin, commanded
translation, object displacement, grasp state -- for all 1200 episodes per arm.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT -- the failures split three ways
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                    FP32       INT8
  SUCCESS                          89.58%     76.08%
  lost grip during transport        3.33%     10.50%
  released, missed the bin          3.17%      6.08%
  blocked -- arm stuck, still held  3.08%      5.92%
  wander (moving, not arriving)     0.83%      1.42%

WANDER -- the class corresponding to "the transport policy cannot navigate" --
is 0.8-1.4%. This independently explains why transport retraining failed twice
over six seeds (Results/transport_retrain_negative.txt): the retrained
component was not the broken one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAUSE 1 -- LOST GRIP, AND NO RECOVERY BRANCH (3.33% FP32 / 10.50% INT8)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  frac_grasped   median 0.09    -- object gone ~9% into the carry
  z_end          median 0.843   -- lying on the table (p10 0.023 = on the floor)
  path_tail      median 0.000   -- nothing moves for the last 50 steps
  tr_steps       300 = horizon-out in every case

The FSM flies an empty gripper for the full PLACE_HORIZON. The cause is a
missing transition, not a policy error:

    elif p == TEST_LIFT:
        if not grasped:  self.phase = GRASP          # recovers
    elif p == TRANSPORT:
        if grasped and over_bin >= RELEASE_TRIG_HOLD: -> RECENTER
        elif tr_steps >= PLACE_HORIZON:              -> FAIL   # no lost-grip path

In some INT8 episodes the empty gripper hovered inside NEAR_TARGET_XY for 264
consecutive steps and still could not trigger, because the trigger requires
`grasped`. -> Fix A.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAUSE 2 -- BLOCKED (3.08% FP32 / 5.92% INT8)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  act_tail   0.90-0.96   full-scale commands
  path_tail  0.003-0.004 no displacement
  frac_grasped 1.000     still holding the object
  z_end      1.41 (FP32) / 1.52 (INT8), table at 0.80
  d_end      0.45-0.47   far outside the 0.18 release radius

The policy commands hard and nothing moves.

NEGATIVE RESULT -- height is NOT the cause, and a carry-height clamp would
have made things worse. Successful episodes reach z_end median 1.257 and max
1.670; the blocked cases at 1.41-1.52 sit inside that range (success p90 1.44).
Carrying high is normal behaviour. A clamp at 1.0 would break every working
episode. Cause pending phase-2 (joint-limit / reach-extent instrumentation).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAUSE 3 -- RELEASED, MISSED THE BIN (3.17% FP32 / 6.08% INT8)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  n_near     exactly 3 in every case -- trigger fires at the first legal instant
  d_end      0.164 (fail) vs 0.161 (success)
  z_end      1.114 (fail) vs 1.257 (success)
  tr_steps   64 FP32 / 18 INT8 (fail) vs 43 / 47 (success)

NEGATIVE RESULT -- "released too early and too high" is WRONG. The failures
release LOWER than the successes and at the same XY error. Neither height nor
XY separates the classes, so a Z gate on the release trigger is unfounded and
points the wrong way.

The surviving signal is timing: INT8 failures trigger after 18 transport steps
against 47 for its successes. Phase-2 instrumentation of RECENTER/DESCEND/OPEN/
RETRACT (phase2_diag.py) localises it -- first evidence is that RECENTER exits
early on `not grasped`, i.e. the object is sheared out of the gripper by the
CARRY_GAIN=6.0 open-loop XY servo. Confirmation pending at 1200 episodes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONSEQUENCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Addressable without retraining: 9.6 of the 10.4 lost FP32 points and 22.5 of
   the 23.9 lost INT8 points are rule-layer defects, not policy defects.
2. The INT8 penalty is specific, not diffuse. It concentrates in grip retention
   under carry (3.33 -> 10.50), i.e. a quantized GRASP net holding worse, not a
   quantized transport net navigating worse. The dead-zone prediction (INT8
   stalls clustering in near-miss) is refuted -- near-miss is not a class.
3. Two of the three "obvious" fixes were refuted by the success-side
   distribution before being implemented. Recording the success distribution,
   not only the failure distribution, is what caught them.

PROTOCOL
1200 episodes per arm = 12 eval seeds x 100, per Results/fsm_rule_layer_sweep.txt.
Artifacts: grasp qat_output_fix/grasp_bi_noqat_int8.tflite,
transport qat_output_bi/place_orig_int8.tflite, tuned rule layer.

================================================================================
PART 2 -- THE THREE FIXES, MEASURED SEPARATELY
1200 episodes per arm (12 eval seeds x 100), paired against the same seeds.
================================================================================

WHY PAIRED STATISTICS
Every arm runs the same 12 seeds with the same spawn sequences, so between-seed
variance -- the 3.3-point sd that made the 4-seed protocol read 3-6 points low
(Results/fsm_rule_layer_sweep.txt) -- cancels. The pooled two-proportion z-test
ignores this pairing and understates every effect here; Fix A reads z = +1.02
("ns") pooled and t(11) = +4.71, sign-test p = 0.0010 paired. Same data.

                        FP32                    INT8
  baseline            89.58%                  76.08%
  Fix A  regrasp      90.33%  +0.75  t=+2.69  77.83%  +1.75  t=+4.71  11up/0down
  Fix B  unjam        89.92%  +0.33  t=+1.77  76.17%  +0.08  t=+1.00   1up/0down
  Fix C  rc_steps 60  89.92%  +0.33  t=+1.30  76.58%  +0.50  t=+3.32   6up/0down

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FIX A -- lost-grip recovery in TRANSPORT.  ADOPTED (+1.75 INT8, p = 0.0010)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Return to GRASP after LOST_GRIP_STEPS=5 consecutive ungrasped steps, bounded by
MAX_REGRASP=2. The recovered episodes appear exactly where the mechanism
predicts -- the 150-250 step band goes 11 -> 27 -- and no seed regressed.

Recovery is partial by nature: 21 of 126 lost-grip episodes, ~17%. The rest are
unrecoverable, not mis-handled -- z_end p10 = 0.023 means the object is on the
floor, outside the workspace.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FIX C -- RC_STEPS 30 -> 60.  ADOPTED (+0.50 INT8, p = 0.031, 0 seeds worse)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RECENTER exits on `ph_steps >= RC_STEPS` at d = 0.074 against RC_TOL = 0.03.
Successes need a median of 24 steps and p90 of exactly 30 -- the ceiling was
binding on the tail of the distribution.

Small because it is capped by a different cause: 38.3% of RECENTER_timeout
episodes are already at a joint limit, against 17.1% of successes. Extra steps
cannot help a pinned arm.

WHY THE ORIGINAL SWEEP MISSED IT
Results/fsm_sweep tested RC_STEPS 20/45 and RC_TOL 0.02/0.04 at 100 episodes:
82, 83, 82, 82 vs 82 baseline -- correctly reported as noise. A 3.9% failure
class is not resolvable at n=100. The sweep was not wrong; it was underpowered,
and no amount of re-sweeping would have found this. Identifying the mechanism
first, then sizing the experiment to the mechanism's incidence, is what worked.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FIX B -- unjam a blocked carry.  NULL (+0.08 INT8), AND THE REASON IS KNOWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The diagnosis is solid. Blocked carries sit AT a joint stop: joint_margin
median -0.000 and 86.4% below 0.02 rad, against 0.432 / 17.1% for successes,
and it is joint 5 (wrist flexion) in 76 of 88 cases.

The REMEDY was wrong, and the probe separates the two cleanly
(Results/fixB_probe, 300 episodes):
    detector fired on 28/300 = 9.3%   -- matches the 7.33% blocked rate
    of those 28, recovered            -- 0
    exhausted MAX_UNJAM=3             -- 16
Detection works. Descending does not free the arm, and the arm re-jams
immediately.

Two candidate reasons, under test:
  * Direction. Blocked carries are more extended than successes (reach_xy 0.313
    vs 0.254) as well as higher (eef_z 1.531 vs 1.256), so retracting toward
    the base may be the right escape where descending is not.
  * Actuation. TRANSPORT executes `a[3:6] = 0.0` every step, discarding the
    policy's rotational command. A pinned wrist joint can only be moved by
    rotational actuation -- the FSM forbids exactly the DOF needed to escape.
    That line is a fixed-spawn-era decision (object rotation pinned at 0.0),
    never revisited for random spawn, where the wrist arrives at varied yaw.

REFUTED ALONG THE WAY -- both before implementation, by the success-side
distribution:
  * carry-height clamp: successes reach z_end 1.67 and median 1.257; the
    blocked cases at 1.41-1.52 are INSIDE the success range.
  * Z gate on the release trigger: failures release LOWER (1.114) than
    successes (1.257) at the same XY error. The gate points the wrong way.
Recording the success distribution alongside the failure distribution is what
caught both. A failure-only analysis would have shipped two regressions.

================================================================================
PART 3 -- THE BLOCKED CLASS IS NOT FIXABLE FROM THE RULE LAYER
Three escape maneuvers, 1200 episodes each. All fire correctly. All recover 0.
================================================================================

                        success        unjam fired     recovered   paired
  baseline INT8       913/1200 76.08%        --            --        --
  unjam descend       (probe, 300 eps)     28 (9.3%)        0        --
  unjam retract       913/1200 76.08%     88 (7.3%)         0     +0.00, 12 ties
  unjam rotate        913/1200 76.08%     88 (7.3%)         0     +0.00, 12 ties
  KEEP_ROTATION=1     285/1200 23.75%        --            --    -52.33, t=-26.8

FINDING 1 -- NO OPEN-LOOP ESCAPE WORKS
Three maneuvers with genuinely distinct actions (verified by unit test):
    descend  xyz [ 0.000  0.000 -0.600]  rot [0.0 0.0 0.0]
    retract  xyz [-0.384 -0.320 -0.300]  rot [0.0 0.0 0.0]
    rotate   xyz [ 0.000  0.000 -0.300]  rot [0.0 0.5 0.0]
The detector fires on the right episodes (7.3-9.3%, against a 7.33% blocked
rate). Every mode recovers exactly 0, and the totals are IDENTICAL to baseline
to the episode, all 12 seeds tied -- the jammed episodes fail under every
escape while the rest are untouched. Once the arm reaches the stop, no
open-loop rule-layer maneuver returns it to a workable configuration.

This is a genuine limitation, not a missing patch. Removing it needs
joint-limit-aware control, or a transport policy that does not drive itself
there -- neither of which lives in the FSM.

FINDING 2 -- `a[3:6] = 0.0` IS LOAD-BEARING, NOT LEGACY  (hypothesis refuted)
The reasoning was: a pinned WRIST joint can only be freed by rotational
actuation, TRANSPORT discards every rotational command, and that line dates
from fixed spawn where object rotation was pinned at 0.0. Passing rotation
through should therefore help.

It is catastrophic: 23.75% against 76.08%, -52.33 points, all 12 seeds worse,
t(11) = -26.8. The transport policy's rotational output is actively harmful and
suppressing it is what makes the stage work at all. The FSM is not withholding
the DOF needed to escape; it is masking a bad output channel.

Worth noting for Sec 8: the decomposed transport policy is only usable because
the rule layer discards 3 of its 7 outputs. That is a real cost of the
decomposition, and it is measurable -- 52 points.

================================================================================
PART 5 -- FIX D, THE POSE GATE.  REJECTED.
The diagnosis is right, the remedy is in the wrong place.
================================================================================

THE FINDING IT WAS BUILT ON (Results/handoff_carry, 2400 episodes)
The rule layer forces a[6] = 1.0 for every step of TRANSPORT, so the grasp
policy exerts NO control over the grip during the carry. INT8 cannot be
"holding worse" -- it is not holding at all. The damage is already present in
the pose handed over.

Measured, and consistent across BOTH precisions:
  median at handoff        carried_ok      dropped
    off_x  (depth)           -0.003        -0.018     6x
    off_z  (height)          +0.001        +0.012    12x
    off_xy                    0.007         0.020     3x
    |rel_yaw|                 42.5 deg      67.8 deg
    grip_w                   0.0474        0.0475    NO SIGNAL
A durable grip is seated deep and level; a drop-prone one is shifted back and
riding high -- a shallow fingertip grasp. Finger opening carries no signal,
which is exactly why _check_grasp and the 3 cm lift certification pass these.

Logistic fit on FP32: AUC 0.915, transferring to INT8 at 0.826. Applying the
FP32 pose->drop map to INT8's ACTUAL handoff poses predicts an 8.49% drop rate
against 3.59% for FP32, so of INT8's +8.54 pt excess the pose shift accounts
for +4.90 pts -- 57%.

off_x/off_y/off_z are obs[7:10], verified identical to R_eef^T (obj - eef).
The gate needs no new sensing and no new computation on device.

THE GATE, AND WHY IT FAILS
Reject a handoff with s[OFF_X] < POSE_OFF_X_MIN or s[OFF_Z] > POSE_OFF_Z_MAX,
set the object down (REGRIP_DOWN then REGRIP_OPEN) and re-pick. The set-down is
required: the policy is deterministic, so re-lifting without releasing
reproduces the identical rejected pose.

  arm (stacked on A+C)     success    vs A+C   t(11)   seeds        sign p   fired
  A+C baseline             78.33%       --       --      --           --       --
  D loose  (-0.020/0.020)  79.42%     +1.08    +1.74   6u/3d/3t     0.5078    5.0%
  D moder  (-0.016/0.012)  79.42%     +1.08    +1.22   6u/5d/1t     1.0000   15.2%

Not significant, and 3-5 seeds regress -- against Fix A (t=4.71, 0 down) and
Fix C (t=3.32, 0 down). The predicted ~4.2 pt ceiling did not materialise.

THE DIAGNOSTIC DETAIL THAT EXPLAINS IT
Episodes where the gate fired succeeded at 70.0% (loose) / 75.8% (moderate),
BELOW the 79.4% overall rate. The gate identifies bad grips correctly; the
re-pick does not produce a good one. Tripling the firing rate (5.0% -> 15.2%)
changed the total by ZERO episodes -- the extra rejections helped as many
episodes as they hurt. That is a gate with no usable operating point, not a
gate that needs tuning.

CONCLUSION -- THE FIX BELONGS IN THE METRIC, NOT THE CONTROLLER
A runtime gate can only reject and retry, and every retry is drawn from the
same deterministic policy that produced the rejected pose. Getting better poses
requires changing what produces them.

The actionable form of this finding is at TRAINING time: lift certification
asks "grasped, and rose 3 cm?" and never inspects where the object sits, so a
policy can certify at 100% while handing over grips that do not survive a
300-step carry. Adding pose quality (off_x depth, off_z level) to the grasp
reward or to the certification criterion selects for grips that survive
transport rather than grips that merely hold for 8 steps.

That also answers the standing question directly: the FP32 grasp model does not
need to approach 100% on the CURRENT metric, because the current metric does
not measure the property that determines downstream success.
