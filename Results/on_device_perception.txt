FULL PERCEPTION ON A PLAIN ESP32
================================
Model: checkpoints/td3_grasp_rand_td3_ln_c2m512_s1/best (FP32 grasp actor)
Protocol: 12 seeds x 50 episodes = 600 episodes/row, random spawn, paired on seed.
Camera: robot0_eye_in_hand 320x240, latch mode, one detection at t=0.

WHY IT IS BETTER, NOT WORSE
---------------------------
Not the detector's detection rate (which is only slightly higher) but the
CONSISTENCY of its error. Raw pose error, held-out seed:

  detector            median   p90     max
  ESP32 C port        20.48   20.80   26.17     <- nearly a constant offset
  OpenCV aruco        20.65   25.46   34.23     <- spreads

Both are ~20.5 mm on average, but the C port's error is almost a pure fixed
extrinsic offset while OpenCV's carries per-detection corner noise. A fixed
offset is exactly what a calibration model removes. So the refit corrector
reaches 0.06 mm where the OpenCV one reached 0.39 mm:

  corrector (held out, n=567)   median   p90    p99    max   >5mm
  on C detections                0.06   0.15   0.36   3.28   0.00%
  on OpenCV detections           0.41   0.71   1.18   1.39   0.00%

  NOTE: the C fit shows a LOWER R2 (0.98/0.97/0.99) than the OpenCV fit
  (0.99/0.99/1.00) despite the far smaller error. That looked like a red
  flag and was checked: there is no hidden tail (p99 0.36 mm, 0% above
  5 mm). R2 is lower because the baseline has almost no variance left to
  explain -- which is the same fact that makes it so correctable.

WHAT RUNS WHERE
---------------
On the ESP32 (at32_perception.h + esp32_apriltag/):
  tag detection, both poses of the planar ambiguity, upright disambiguation,
  extrinsic calibration, residual corrector, the object-pose block of the
  state vector, the grasp/place policies, the FSM.
From the PC (IMG_MSG):
  the ROI pixels and the camera-to-world transform. The transform is FORWARD
  KINEMATICS, not perception -- a real arm's controller knows where its wrist
  camera is.

Perception runs ONCE per episode, at t=0. That is what makes the residual
correctable: the arm is at its home pose, so the camera pose is fixed and
detected->true is one deterministic map. After the grasp the object rides
with the gripper, so its pose is recomposed from the latched grip offset.

MEMORY -- SETTLED ON HARDWARE, AND IT DID NOT FIT AT FIRST
----------------------------------------------------------
The board reports, at the moment of detection:
    free 276536 B, largest block 110580 B
The 222x193 build needed more and PANICKED:
    Guru Meditation Error: Core 1 panic'ed (StoreProhibited)
    EXCVADDR: 0x00000c00
-- a write through a NULL pointer at a small offset. Upstream apriltag
never checks its mallocs, so out-of-memory is a panic rather than an error.
The board then reboots, the PC re-syncs, and the episodes get scored on the
ground-truth pose the PC sent. Perception failing SILENTLY and scoring
19/20 is the trap; hil_main now reports what perception actually did, and
says so loudly when the answer is nothing.

Three changes brought it inside the budget:
    wrap the received frame instead of copying it       42 KB
    AT_MIN_REGION 25 -> 60 (noise regions never
      clustered, so their zarrays never allocated)      15 KB
    crop 222x193 -> 210x181 @ (110,0)             24 + 5 KB
Peak 283 -> 259 KB on the host; margin ~+14 -> ~+50 KB. Only 43 blocks are
live at peak, so allocator overhead is negligible: the failure was total
size, not fragmentation or per-block cost.

A separate mismatch mattered as much: AT_CLUSTERMAP_FRAC and AT_MEM_CHUNK
defaulted to upstream 0.2/2048 with the deployment values passed as
"optional" -D flags, and the Arduino IDE cannot pass flags to a library.
The DEVICE was building a 321 KB peak while every desktop figure was the
298 KB flagged build. Deployment values are now the defaults.

THE REAL BUDGET IS 211 KB, NOT 281 KB
-------------------------------------
    [perc] start: getFreeHeap 281376 | 8BIT free 215940 largest 110580
ESP.getFreeHeap() sums EVERY heap cap, including ~65 KB of
32-bit-access-only IRAM that cannot back the uint8 images and uint16
unionfind arrays the detector allocates. The pool that constrains it is
MALLOC_CAP_8BIT: 210.9 KB, largest block 108 KB. Two predicted fits were
made against getFreeHeap and both were wrong.

COST OF FITTING (12 seeds x 50 episodes, paired on seed):
    222x193, region 25   91.00%   x86 peak 283 KB   PANICS
    210x181, region 60   90.00%   x86 peak 259 KB   PANICS
    190x170, region 60   89.17%   x86 peak 220 KB   fits, ~24 KB spare
    190x170 vs 222x193: -1.83 pts, SE 0.67, 95% CI [-3.31, -0.35]
      -- SIGNIFICANT. Fitting a plain ESP32 costs real accuracy.
    190x170 vs 210x181: -0.83 pts, 95% CI [-2.89, +1.23], not significant
Coverage of the n=2281 detection distribution falls 94.5% -> 91.3%, and
sim detection 139/200 -> 135/200 at an unchanged 20.51 mm.
Detection is unchanged upstream of the task too: 43/60 recorded frames
either way, and 139/200 sim resets at 20.52 mm against 20.51 mm.

So the deployable configuration is 89.17%: level with the external
agentview camera (89.67%), still above the OpenCV wrist path (87.67%), and
~5 points under the ground-truth ceiling. The headline is no longer "on-
device perception beats the PC camera" -- that was true only for crops the
hardware cannot actually run. What survives is that full AprilTag
perception runs on a plain ESP32 with no PSRAM at a measured cost of 1.8
points against the same detector given unlimited memory.

2. TIME. The detector has never been timed on hardware. Inference is 0.26 ms
   and the corrector is trivial, but AprilTag quad detection on a 111x96
   working image at 240 MHz is unknown -- plausibly tens to hundreds of ms.
   It runs once per episode, so a slow detection costs startup latency, not
   control rate. esp32_bridge.send_image() waits a fixed 2 s for this
   reason; shorten it once the real figure is in the debug log.
