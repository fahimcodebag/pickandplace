DOES THE GRASP POLICY ALIGN GRIPPER YAW TO THE OBJECT?
======================================================
Model: checkpoints/td3_grasp_rand_td3_ln_c2m512_s1/best
6 seeds x 50 episodes = 300 grasps, random spawn (includes z-rotation).
Alignment error sampled at the moment _check_grasp first returns true, folded
into [-45,45] deg because parallel jaws are symmetric under 90 deg:
0 deg = jaws square to the object's faces, 45 deg = jaws on its corners.

  |alignment error|  median 20.0   mean 20.9   p90 40.0   max 44.6 deg
  a policy that IGNORES yaw gives mean 22.5 (uniform over 0-45)

  histogram   0-9   9-18  18-27 27-36 36-45
               76    59    57    57    51
  chi2 vs uniform = 5.9 on 4 df (9.49 = p0.05) -> consistent with uniform

THE POLICY DOES NOT ALIGN YAW. The distribution is statistically
indistinguishable from ignoring object yaw entirely. There is a mild excess
in the 0-9 deg bin (76 against 60 expected) but it does not reach
significance at n=300.

WHY THIS MATTERS
The task geometry says yaw should not affect REACHABILITY: the bread is
44x48 mm, diagonal 65 mm, inside the Panda's ~80 mm opening at any yaw. It
should affect GRIP QUALITY -- jaws on two flat faces have a large contact
patch, jaws on opposite corners have almost none. The policy nevertheless
grasps at ~95%, so either grip quality matters less than expected for a
near-cubic object, or the remaining failures are concentrated in the
misaligned tail and nobody has looked.

It also means the old 28% collapse under a flipped pose (apriltag_sim.py,
the reason the upright prior exists) was NOT caused by losing yaw alignment
-- there is none to lose. The likelier cause is the wrong quaternion pushing
the actor off its training distribution, or corrupting obj_to_eef_quat.

NOT MEASURED: whether misalignment predicts the mid-carry drops. Two
attempts failed. The first counted a single ungrasped frame as a drop, which
_check_grasp's flicker turns into ~80% nonsense. The second used the FSM's
own 5-consecutive-frame rule and still reported 83%, because the harness
drives the grasp policy ALONE with no FSM: after the grasp nothing takes
over, so the policy acts past its job and releases. That question needs the
full FSM loop (fsm_sim.py) and is still open.

FOLLOW-UPS THIS OPENS
  - An explicit yaw-alignment step before closing, in the FSM, is cheap and
    currently unexploited. Whether it helps depends on the drop question
    above.
  - Since yaw is not used, feeding a CONSTANT canonical quaternion in place
    of the detected one may cost nothing -- which would remove the two-fold
    ambiguity disambiguation, the upright prior, and let the ~33% of
    detections that min_up currently rejects be accepted instead.
