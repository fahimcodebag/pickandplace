The bi_s0 gain does not transfer.  The prediction was falsified in the direction
it named as falsifying: a significant loss.

  READING.  With a[3:6] = 0 the OSC orientation goal stays at the grasp-time
  attitude and the PD keeps pulling the gripper back to it.  With the anchor the
  goal is reset to the measured attitude every step, so attitude error is never
  corrected: a steep grasp drifts further (13 vs 6 deg), and RECENTER, which servos
  position only, releases the box ~0.16 m off target.  On bi_s0 the same freedom
  paid because holding the grasp attitude drove joint 5 into its stop in 3.2% of
  episodes; c2m512_s1 pins in 0.4% but hands off at >= 60 deg in 19%.
  UNTESTED CANDIDATE: re-anchor only while joint 5 is near its stop.

CONSEQUENCES
  * The headline stands as recorded: random INT8 95.33 / FP32 94.92, fixed INT8
    99.83 / FP32 100.0 -- measured with the anchor off, which the firmware matches.
  * Do NOT mirror ROT_ANCHOR_EPS into pick_and_place_INT8_FSM.ino for c2m512_s1.
  * fsm_sim.py defaults to ROT_ANCHOR_EPS = 1e-6 (since 4932118).  Any evaluation
    of c2m512_s1 must pass --rot-anchor-eps 0, or it reads ~2.5-2.75 pts low on
    random spawn.  No such evaluation was found in the repo's scripts.
  * The bi_s0 result (orientation_anchor.txt) stands for bi_s0.  The anchor's net
    effect depends on how often the grasp drives joint 5 to its stop: 3.17% of
    episodes for bi_s0, 0.42% for c2m512_s1.  "Prevention, not escape" was right
    about the class and wrong to generalise as a free fix.
