================================================================================
THE ORIENTATION ANCHOR -- a controller-interface defect, not a policy defect
The "blocked at a joint limit" class was never fixable from the rule layer
because it was never IN the rule layer.  One constant recovers it.
================================================================================

    + release radius 0.18->0.08 82.3% -> 89.2%  + 6.83  t(11)= +5.30 12up/0down

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
robosuite/controllers/osc.py, set_goal():

    bools = [0.0 if math.isclose(e, 0.0) else 1.0 for e in scaled_delta[3:]]
    if sum(bools) > 0.0 or set_ori is not None:
        self.goal_ori = set_goal_orientation(...)      # CONDITIONAL
    self.goal_pos = set_goal_position(...)             # UNCONDITIONAL

Position re-anchors to the measured eef every policy step (goal = current +
delta).  Orientation updates ONLY when the rotational delta is nonzero.

So `a[3:6] = 0.0` does not mean "no orientation command".  It freezes goal_ori
at whatever absolute WORLD attitude was last commanded -- for the FSM, back in
GRASP, i.e. whatever arbitrary yaw the object happened to be picked at -- and
the orientation PD (kp=150) then fights to hold that world attitude across the
entire 0.65 m traverse from pick bin (y=-0.25) to place bin (y=+0.28).

Panda joint 5 absorbs the arc.  Its range is [-0.02, 3.75]; every other joint
is +-2.9 or wider.  It saturates:

  stalled carry windows   q5 = 3.753   against the 3.75 stop
  limit proximity (0 = at a stop, normalised by range)
        FAILED episodes   0.007        closest joint = j5 in 3670/4135 samples
        SUCCESS episodes  0.070
  motion achieved / commanded    failed 4.6%   success 15.5%
  windows moving <5 mm in 12 steps   failed 67%   success 4%
  contacts (arm or box vs bins)      NONE in either group

Nothing in the loop bounds joint angles.  OSC consumes Cartesian deltas;
nullspace_torques regulates posture toward initial_joint but never reads
jnt_range.  The only thing stopping a joint is MuJoCo's mechanical stop.  The
arm jams silently, still holding the object, commanding full scale, and the FSM
reads the stall as convergence.

WHY THE FIX IS 1e-6 AND NOT A ROTATION
  1e-6 scales to ~5e-7 rad.  It moves nothing.  Its only effect is to flip the
  isclose() branch so goal_ori re-anchors to the current attitude each step.
  The ordering confirms the mechanism rather than the result:
      KEEP_ROTATION=1 (large policy rotations)     -52.33
      a[3:6] = 0      (frozen absolute goal_ori)   baseline
      a[3:6] = 1e-3   (tiny rotation + re-anchor)  +18.3
      a[3:6] = 1e-6   (re-anchor only, no motion)  +24.2
  Less commanded rotation, more benefit.  KEEP_ROTATION stays REJECTED; it
  masks a bad policy output channel and that finding is unchanged.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THIS IS THE CLASS PART 3 COULD NOT FIX, AND THE ATTRIBUTION WAS CORRECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Results/transport_stall_diagnosis.txt measured the blocked class at 3.08% FP32,
found joint 5 pinned in 76/88 cases, tried three open-loop escapes at 1200
episodes each, and recovered exactly 0 from every one.  Its conclusion --
"Once the arm reaches the stop, no open-loop rule-layer maneuver returns it to
a workable configuration.  Removing it needs joint-limit-aware control, or a
transport policy that does not drive itself there -- neither of which lives in
the FSM" -- was right.

The bread gain here is +2.75 against a 3.08% class.  The fix collects ~90% of
exactly the failures that campaign isolated.  It is not a new capability; it is
the same class, addressed one layer down.  Prevention, not escape.

WHY NO NULLSPACE METHOD CAN WORK HERE -- a structural result worth keeping.
A 7-DOF arm against a 6-DOF task leaves exactly ONE nullspace dimension: the
elbow swivel.  Joint 5 is wrist flexion, which DETERMINES the task orientation,
so (I - Jbar J) projects out precisely the component that would move it.  This
applies identically to posture regulation, to gradient-projection limit
avoidance, and to CLIK's (I - J+J) q0 term.  Adding a PID integral does not
help either: the steady-state error is a KINEMATIC constraint, not a
disturbance, so integral action winds up and saturates against a mechanical
stop.  The feedback path was never missing -- OSC is already a closed-loop
task-space PD at kp=150, kv=2*sqrt(kp).

The lever is not more redundancy, it is choosing which task dimensions to
constrain.  Releasing the orientation task is what freed the wrist.

WHAT THE FIX EXPOSED -- staging becomes real, and redundancy stops mattering
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                              before fix          after fix
  leg1 exits by tolerance     50%                 70%
  stage x                     +0.077 +- 0.042     +0.098 +- 0.019
  stage y                     -0.086 +- 0.086     -0.050 +- 0.047
  stage z                     +1.228 +- 0.103     +1.233 +- 0.067
  per-seed spread (3 seeds)   62 / 75 / 40        85 / 82 / 82

The staging pose was never common: legs time out (WP_LEG_CAP) and hand over
from wherever the arm is, WP_TOL tests xy only, and a[2] is ascent-only so
WP_Z is a floor.  Much of the outcome variance attributed to staging was
actually the frozen goal_ori, which depended on the arbitrary grasp yaw.

A PRESET JOINT CONFIGURATION AT STAGING IS NO LONGER WORTH IT.  Measured after
the fix, 240 episodes, joint angles at leg-2 entry, success vs failure:
    j0 d'=0.12   j1 d'=0.22   j2 d'=0.18   j3 d'=0.07
    j4 d'=0.10   j5 d'=0.22   j6 d'=0.11
No joint separates the classes.  The spread is still enormous (j4 sd 1.11 rad,
j6 sd 0.87 rad) and the arm reaches the bin equally well from all of it.  j5
now sits at 3.524 +- 0.219, ~0.23 rad clear of the stop, against 3.753 pinned
before.  Driving to a fixed joint pose would standardise a variable that no
longer predicts anything, at the cost of a controller swap (JOINT_POSITION
needs its own IK, changes the closed-loop dynamics the grasp policy trained
under, and re-characterises every OSC_POSE number in the results table).
It WAS the right fix for the pre-fix regime.  It is not needed now.

THE RESIDUAL ~10% IS A SCORING ARTIFACT, NOT A CONTROL FAILURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
600 episodes at the fixed config, cereal.  robosuite scores 89.7%.  All 62
failures fall in ONE class, "released, not placed", and:

  where the box physically ends up (after 60 settle steps)
      success  537 inside target compartment,  1 elsewhere
      failure   60 inside target compartment,  2 elsewhere     <- 97% correct

  release accuracy   success 0.0124 +- 0.0131   failure 0.0120 +- 0.0129
  release height     success 1.211  +- 0.083    failure 1.214  +- 0.079

Identical to four decimals.  What separates them is the criterion, from
robosuite/environments/manipulation/pick_place.py, not_in_bin():

    and self.bin2_pos[2] < obj_pos[2] < self.bin2_pos[2] + 0.1    # 0.80..0.90

  final resting z    success 0.8914 +- 0.0123   failure 0.9053 +- 0.0036
                     above 0.90:  17.5%                    95.2%

A CEREAL BOX LYING FLAT ON THE BIN FLOOR RESTS AT EXACTLY 0.90 -- the exclusive
upper bound.  It scores as placed only if it settles a few mm low or the check
is sampled mid-bounce.  The whole success/failure split is ~1.4 cm of resting
height at a boundary the object cannot reliably clear.  Bread rests at ~0.825
and is unaffected, so every bread-vs-cereal comparison in this project is
confounded by it.

This also explains why RT_STEPS 12 -> 60 -> 120 was bit-identical, twice: the
box is not still in transit, it has SETTLED above the window, permanently.
Waiting cannot help.  Physical placement for cereal is 598/600 = 99.7%.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAVEATS -- read before rewriting the headline table
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The bread baseline here reads 88.7%, against the 90.67% recorded for A+C.
   Only --regrasp and --rc-steps 60 were applied, not necessarily every tuned
   rule-layer constant from that campaign.  The PAIRED deltas are valid (both
   arms ran the identical config) but the absolute cells must be re-measured
   at the exact recorded configuration before the table is edited.
2. INT8 is UNTESTED.  The blocked class was larger there (5.92% vs 3.08%), so
   the gain may be larger, but that is a prediction, not a measurement.
3. "Came to rest in the compartment" is OUR criterion, with a settle phase the
   environment does not run.  Report both numbers and state the artifact; do
   not silently substitute ours for robosuite's.
4. NOT mirrored to pick_and_place_INT8_FSM.ino.  The .ino zeroes rotation the
   same way.  Whether it matters on hardware depends on the real controller's
   goal handling -- the isclose() branch is robosuite-specific, but holding a
   fixed world attitude through a large arc is a physical error and a real
   Panda's joint 5 has the same one-sided range.  check_fsm_sync.py must gain
   ROT_ANCHOR_EPS when that mirror happens.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
METHOD NOTE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Five refutations against two adopted fixes.  The failure statistics pointed at
none of it -- every hypothesis drawn from the failure distribution (reach,
collision, carry height, release height, settle window, staging pose, nullspace
posture, limit-aware nullspace) was refuted.  The mechanism was found by
reading osc.py after the nullspace theory collapsed.

Sweeps here run as one process per (arm, seed) under xargs -P 28.  MuJoCo will
not parallelise a single environment, but seeds are embarrassingly parallel:
3400 episodes finished in the wall time one 100-episode job takes.  The earlier
sequential form of the same sweep would have run ~2.5 hours at 3% CPU.
