================================================================================
APRILTAG PERCEPTION END-TO-END: THREE IMPLEMENTATION BUGS, THEN 28% -> 68%
The camera goes blind at grasp in every configuration. That is survivable --
a held object does not need to be seen -- but only with the right propagation.
================================================================================

WHAT WAS ALREADY BUILT
apriltag_sim.py (tag injection + OpenCV DICT_APRILTAG_36h11 + solvePnP),
perception_wrapper.py (periodic perception, truth/recompute/frozen),
sweep_perception.py, validate_tag_perception.py.

TWO SCOPE PROBLEMS WITH THE EXISTING EVIDENCE
1. validate_tag_perception.py detects ONE frame per episode, at reset, with the
   arm parked at home. That is the easiest case for a fixed camera and the
   hardest for a wrist camera, and says nothing about detection during motion.
2. sweep_perception.py:evaluate scores info["grasp_success"] with a 200-step cap
   running the GRASP ACTOR ALONE -- no FSM, no transport, no place. Its headline
   ("flat to a 20x update period at 10 mm noise") therefore covers only the
   phase where the tag is visible. It was never evidence about the carry.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 1: DETECTION DIES AT GRASP, IN EVERY CAMERA CONFIGURATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Real detector, real FSM, measured per phase during episodes (tag_inflight.py):

  phase        agentview 1280x960     eye_in_hand 640x480
  GRASP          65%  11.6 mm            29%   3.6 mm
  TEST_LIFT       0%     --               0%     --
  TRANSPORT       0%     --               0%     --
  RECENTER       19%   9.3 mm             0%     --
  OPEN           33%   8.9 mm             0%     --

Once the object is held the gripper occludes the tag. This is geometry, not
resolution, and it does not improve with a better camera.

THE ARCHITECTURAL CONSEQUENCE
A held object's pose RELATIVE TO THE GRIPPER is constant, and the gripper pose
is exact from joint encoders. So it does not need to be seen -- it needs to
have been seen ONCE, at grasp. Hence mode="latch": recompute until grasped,
then latch object->gripper and propagate world = eef (*) latched.
"recompute" is actively WRONG after grasp: it holds a stale WORLD pose while
the arm carries the object away from it, so the relative block diverges.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 2: THREE IMPLEMENTATION BUGS, WORTH 40 POINTS TOGETHER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. RESOLUTION. 320x240 and 640x480 gave 0/5 detections at agentview -- the tag
   renders at ~10 px, below AprilTag's decode threshold. 960x720 detects but at
   61 mm error; 1280x960 gives 10 mm.

2. PLANAR POSE AMBIGUITY. solvePnP with IPPE_SQUARE returns ONE of two poses
   that project almost identically. Measured: orientation median 3.8 deg but
   p95 91.3 deg, with 3/15 detections flipped ~90 deg. Fixed with
   solvePnPGeneric: reject the flipped solution by a physics prior (the object
   rests flat, so the tag normal points up, min_up=0.30), then choose among
   survivors by reprojection error. Scoring both together instead
   (up - k*err) fixes orientation but degrades position -- measured, and
   recorded in the code comment.
   After: orientation p95 4.7 deg, 0/25 flips.

3. FRAME/OFFSET. The detector returned the TAG pose while the observation
   expects the OBJECT CENTRE. Decomposing the residual identified it as fixed
   in the WORLD frame (sd 11.0/3.4/10.8 mm) rather than the tag frame
   (12.2/13.5/10.8), i.e. a camera-extrinsics artifact, not tag mounting -- so
   it is corrected in world coordinates. The z term also shows the true tag
   height is ~13 mm above the object centre, not the 26 mm the z_offset default
   assumed.

DETECTOR AFTER THE FIXES (agentview 1280x960, 25 resets)
  detection 25/25 | position median 11.2 mm p95 25.8 | angle median 2.9 deg p95 4.7

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 3: END-TO-END, FULL FSM (25 episodes, period 5, c2m512_s1)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  mode                 success
  truth (ceiling)       100.0%   (8 episodes)
  latch  BEFORE fixes    28.0%
  recompute BEFORE       24.0%
  latch  AFTER fixes     68.0%
  recompute AFTER        44.0%

latch beats recompute by 24 points, confirming the propagation argument. The
28%/24% figures were NOT evidence against AprilTag perception -- they were
three compounding implementation bugs.

STATUS AND WHAT IS NOT YET KNOWN
A 32-point gap to ground truth remains at 68%. Candidate causes, none yet
separated: 11.2 mm position noise; detection gaps during GRASP (65% at
agentview) held over 5-step periods; and the latch being taken from whatever
pose was current at the grasp instant, which may be stale or noisy.
The truth baseline is only 8 episodes and needs matching at 25+.
Nothing here is at the 1200-episode protocol used elsewhere in this campaign.

     7    90.0%    72.0%   -18.0

HARNESS VALIDATION: truth reads 94.0% against c2m512_s1's independently
measured 94.92% FP32 held-out. tag_e2e.py builds the 46-D observation by hand,
so this agreement says the harness is sound and the 15 points are perception,
not integration error.

CORRECTIONS TO EARLIER FIGURES IN THIS FILE
The "100% truth ceiling" was 8 episodes and is wrong; at 25-50 episodes it is
88-94%. The perception gap is therefore 15 points, not the 32 first reported.
Pairing matters here: an unpaired comparison is hopeless, because latch alone
spans 72-88% across seeds -- as large as the effect being measured.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TWO THINGS THAT DO NOT EXPLAIN THE REMAINING GAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UPDATE RATE IS NOT THE BOTTLENECK. period 1 and period 5 both give 68% on
seed 7 -- detecting 5x more often buys nothing. Useful for the hardware spec:
an ESP32-CAM at a few frames per second is not the limiting factor, so effort
belongs on pose ACCURACY, not frame rate.

MEDIAN-LATCH DOES NOT HELP (NEGATIVE RESULT). Latching the median of the last
5 detections instead of the single current one: 76.5% vs 79.0%, -2.5 points,
paired t=-1.99 p=0.1411, 0/3 seeds up. The rationale was that the object is
static before the grasp so detections are independent samples of a fixed
quantity. The measurement says otherwise, and the reason is visible in the
per-phase data:

  detection position error at reset (arm parked)   11.2 mm median
  detection position error during episodes         ~21 mm median

Pose error nearly DOUBLES once the arm is moving. The approaching gripper
progressively occludes the tag and corrupts the corner estimates, so the recent
history is not a set of clean independent samples but a sequence of
increasingly occluded views -- averaging them mixes in worse data.
(Error does NOT correlate with gripper-object distance, corr +0.10, as expected
for a FIXED camera whose view does not change with the arm.)

WHERE THE 15 POINTS ACTUALLY LIVE -- STILL OPEN
Not update rate, not the latch instant. The leading remaining candidate is
per-detection accuracy during the approach (~21 mm under partial occlusion),
which is a camera placement/resolution question rather than a filtering one.
Untested: a second camera for redundancy, a larger tag, or tag placement that
the gripper does not cover on approach.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TWO CAMERAS MAKE IT WORSE -- AND THE REASON UNIFIES THREE NEGATIVE RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Camera survey at 1280x960 (20-30 resets each): agentview 15/15 at 12.0 mm,
robot0_robotview 15/15 at 15.6 mm, birdview 13/15 at 77.2 mm, frontview 0/15.
Both usable cameras are STATIC -- measured 0.00 mm and 0.00 deg drift over 30
steps of arm motion -- so extrinsics are exact constants. No pose estimation,
no IMU, nothing to compensate.

END-TO-END, paired on 4 seeds, 50 episodes per cell:
  seed    truth    1 cam   2cam select   2cam average
     7    90.0%    72.0%      68.0%         70.0%
    31    98.0%    88.0%      82.0%         84.0%
    47    92.0%    76.0%      62.0%         70.0%
    89    96.0%    80.0%      74.0%         78.0%
  mean    94.0%    79.0%      71.5%         75.5%

  2cam-avg vs 1cam   -3.5 pts  t=-3.66 p=0.0354  0/4 seeds up
  2cam-sel vs 1cam   -7.5 pts  t=-3.38 p=0.0430  0/4 seeds up

FIRST ERROR: SELECTING BY REPROJECTION ERROR. Reprojection error is a
pixel-space residual computed inside each camera's own image and is NOT
comparable across views with different geometry. The rule picked the weaker
camera 60% of the time and produced 15.0 mm median -- worse than agentview
alone at 10.4 mm. Averaging instead gives 8.0 mm, better than either input.

SECOND ERROR, AND THE REAL ONE: BETTER POSES, WORSE TASK. Averaging is more
accurate both at reset (8.0 vs 10.4 mm) AND in-episode (7.8 vs 10.7 mm), yet
loses 3.5 points end-to-end. The measurement that explains it:

  in-episode, pre-grasp:  agentview 10.7 mm | robotview 17.2 mm | avg 7.8 mm
  BOTH cameras saw the tag on only 18/35 = 51% of ticks

On the other 49% there is no average -- only whichever camera survived. When
that is robotview the policy gets a FRESH 17.2 mm pose, whereas single-camera
operation gets NO detection and zero-order-holds its last good pose instead.
The second camera therefore trades "hold an accurate stale pose" for "use a
fresh worse one", and this policy prefers the stale one.

THE UNIFYING PRINCIPLE
Three independent negative results share one cause:
  period 1 == period 5 (68% both)   staleness is nearly free
  median-latch          -2.5 pts    mixes worse samples into a good one
  two cameras           -3.5 pts    substitutes a worse camera when blocked
Before the grasp the object is STATIONARY, so a stale measurement costs almost
nothing while a noisy one costs directly. The winning strategy is to take the
single most accurate measurement available and HOLD it -- not to fuse, refresh
or average. Every optimisation attempted here violated that principle, which is
why all three failed. This also predicts that a QUALITY GATE on single-camera
detections (reject high-reprojection poses, keep the previous one) should help
where fusion did not. Untested.

WHAT IS AND IS NOT A LEVER, ALL MEASURED
  tag size      AT THE CEILING. 4.4 cm tag on a 4.0x4.8x4.9 cm object already
                overhangs it; 2.8 cm halves detection and quadruples error.
  resolution    PAST RETURNS. 960->1280 is the real step (23.8 -> 10.1 mm);
                1600x1200 is flat, and that is the OV2640/ESP32-CAM ceiling.
  frame rate    NOT A LEVER. period 1 == period 5.
  filtering     NOT A LEVER. median-latch is negative.
  multi-camera  NOT A LEVER. negative under both fusion rules.
  camera motion NOT THE PROBLEM. the camera is provably static.

BEST CONFIGURATION FOUND: ONE camera (agentview 1280x960), mode=latch,
single most recent detection, zero-order hold. 79.0% against a 94.0% ceiling.
The 15-point gap is the measured cost of real AprilTag perception and is
dominated by per-detection error under gripper occlusion during the approach.
