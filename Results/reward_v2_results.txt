REWARD V2 (A+C+D): MECHANICALLY SUCCESSFUL, END-TO-END A WASH
================================================================================

METHOD NOTE -- this is the transferable part
Each fix was priced on 400 RECORDED episodes before any training, using
reward_audit.py and per-term accounting emitted from inside _grasp_reward
itself. The predicted accumulated value was then compared against a live run of
the implemented reward:

  outcome      predicted   measured
  CERTIFIED        336.0      336.0
  slip              90.2       90.2
  flicker          -17.5      -17.5
  touched          -80.7      -80.7
  never gripped    -85.9      -85.9

All five classes matched to 0.1. Every claim below was therefore known before
an hour of GPU time was spent, and the one failure was diagnosable.

WHAT THE AUDIT FOUND ON THE INCUMBENT
1. The success bonus was min 150.0, max 150.0, sd 0.000 across 290 certified
   grasps while quality varied 8-fold (lift rise 38-318mm, alignment
   0.01-0.99). corr(total reward, alignment) = +0.05. The reward was BLIND to
   grasp quality: no gradient existed past the certification bar.
2. Flicker-farming was suppressed but not dead -- it scored 150.5, SECOND-BEST
   of all outcomes and above slip. Net +8.43 per held step made farming beat a
   clean success at 26 held steps of 200; the policy already reached 14, a
   margin of 1.79x. The earlier W_GRASP_SUCCESS 20 -> 150 override had only
   moved break-even to 39, never closing it.
3. Parking near the object netted +54.1: P_IDLE (-0.4) never covered W_REACH
   (up to +1.0).

RESULT 1 -- (C) AND (D) WORKED, EXACTLY AS PRICED
  flicker per 50 episodes   17-22  ->  5-6
  drops per episode, first 300 -> last 300
      s0  0.41 -> 0.13    s1  0.48 -> 0.31    s2  0.65 -> 0.25
  margin certified/best-failure   1.62x -> 3.73x
  flicker break-even              26 held steps -> impossible

RESULT 2 -- (A)'s ALIGNMENT HALF IS INERT
  corner prevalence   23.5% -> 25.5 / 25.5 / 22.2
  mean angle from a flat face  18.9 -> 20.3 / 20.0 / 18.4 deg
The graded bonus created a real, correctly-signed gradient (corr with
alignment +0.05 -> +0.85) that the policy simply did not climb.

RESULT 3 -- QUALITY WAS REDISTRIBUTED, NOT INCREASED
  success by grip angle      flat 0-15      corner 30-45
    gripfix_s2                  92.6%          70.2%
    rv2_s2                      87.4%          80.9%
                             z=-1.80         z=+1.86
Corner grips gained 10.7 points, flat grips lost 5.2. This is the LIFT-RISE
half of (A) working as specified -- it pays for higher lift regardless of
angle, so marginal grips became more liftable -- while the angle distribution
never shifted. The two effects very nearly cancel.

RESULT 4 -- NET END-TO-END UNCHANGED (400 episodes each)
  gripfix_s2 (incumbent)   335/400  83.8%
  rv2_s2                   338/400  84.5%   z=+0.29  ns
  rv2_s0                   320/400  80.0%   z=-1.38  ns
  rv2_s1                   313/400  78.2%   z=-1.99  significantly worse
Grasp stage: rv2 mean 77.2% vs gripfix 79.2%.

DECISION
gripfix_s2 is KEPT as the pipeline artifact. rv2_s2 is statistically
indistinguishable (+0.7 points), has a weaker grasp stage, and switching on
that basis would repeat the best-of-N selection error corrected earlier.
gripfix_s2 is additionally confirmed on held-out eval seeds.

STANDING CONCLUSION ON WRIST ANGLE
Three interventions have now failed to move grip ANGLE through reward
magnitude: the align_grip multiplier (measurably hurt), and the graded bonus
(inert on alignment). The policy takes the reward loss rather than rotate.
Reward magnitude appears to be the wrong lever; see reward_v2_followup for
whether the angle is controllable at all.
