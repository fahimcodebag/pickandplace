================================================================================
MONOLITHIC CONTROL (v7/v8): CORNER GRIPS WERE NEVER THE PROBLEM
The old monolithic policy certifies 98.7% of grasps with the SAME corner rate.
================================================================================

WHY THIS TEST
Four reward interventions failed to reduce corner grips in the decomposed grasp
stage. Before concluding that alignment is unlearnable, the obvious control was
the user's earlier MONOLITHIC model: same task, same 46-D observation, same
OSC_POSE action space, same native full-rotation spawn -- but 2.2M actor
parameters against the decomposed stage's 5.2k, and 30M timesteps of training.
Checkpoints live outside the repo at /home/fahim/Thesis_fahim/checkpoints/.

train_v8.py's own docstring records the motivation for decomposing:
  "The v7 agent grasps and lifts perfectly but jitters after lifting."

MATCHED-CRITERIA COMPARISON
Identical to the decomposed stage: 8 consecutive grasped steps, then a 20-step
scripted lift, >=3cm rise, still grasped, 200-step horizon, native spawn.

  model                    grasped   lift-cert   corner%   flat%   mean diag
  v7 final (seed 7)          99.3%      98.7%     26.5%   41.3%      19.7
  v7 final (seed 123)        98.7%      98.0%     24.7%   40.9%      19.8
  v8 best   (seed 7)         97.3%      96.3%     20.5%   49.3%      17.9
  decomposed gripfix_s2         --      79.2%     23.5%   43.8%      18.9

TWO FINDINGS, THE SECOND OVERTURNS EARLIER WORK

1. THE MONOLITHIC GRASP STAGE IS ~20 POINTS BETTER.
   98.7% vs 79.2% lift-certified, reproduced across two eval seeds. The user's
   recollection that the monolithic model "could grasp from random spawn pretty
   well" is correct and understated.

2. IT DOES NOT ALIGN ITS WRIST EITHER -- AND DOES NOT NEED TO.
   Corner-grip prevalence is statistically the same (26.5% vs 23.5%), mean
   angle from a flat face is the same (19.7 vs 18.9 deg). But its corner grips
   SUCCEED:

     lift certification by grip angle (v7 final, 600 episodes)
       flat   0-15 deg   n=244   100.0%
       mid   15-30 deg   n=198    99.5%
       corner 30-45 deg  n=152    98.0%

     decomposed gripfix_s2, end-to-end by the same angle buckets
       flat 92.6%   mid 81.7%   corner 70.2%

CORRECTION TO Results/handoff_diagnostic.txt
That document reported corner grips as costing 20-26 points end-to-end,
monotonic across three policies, p<5e-3, and treated the angle as the causal
mechanism worth fixing. The correlation was real, but it is NOT causal: a
stronger policy lifts corner grips at 98.0%. Grip angle was a PROXY for grip
quality inside weak policies, not the constraint. Four reward interventions
(align_grip multiplier, reward-v2 graded bonus, dense alignment penalty, and
the wrist-alignment arm) were therefore aimed at the wrong variable. The
critic-blindness measurement (corr(Q, alignment) ~ 0 across six critics) still
stands as a fact; it simply no longer matters.

WHAT THE REAL GAP IS
Not angle. The decomposed 5.2k actor grips worse than the 2.2M actor at every
angle, corners most visibly because they are the hardest case. This is a
capacity/optimisation gap in the grasp policy itself, not a reward-shaping
problem -- which is consistent with every reward fix failing to move it.

CHECKPOINT NOTE
v7's "best" file is WORSE than its final (54.0% vs 97.3% lift-cert): best_score
was recorded against v7's own carry-inclusive reward, not grasp quality. v8
shows the opposite (best 96.3%, final 63.3%) -- it decayed after its peak.
Neither file name can be trusted without measuring.

NEXT DIRECTION
Distillation. The monolithic policy is 2.2M parameters and cannot run on an
ESP32, but it is a 98.7% teacher for exactly the sub-task the 5.2k student is
weak at. Behaviour cloning / DAgger from v7 into the 64x32 grasp actor targets
the measured gap directly, and compression-for-MCU is already the thesis's
subject. Untested.
