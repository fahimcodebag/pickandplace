================================================================================
GRIP ROBUSTNESS UNDER SCRIPTED DISTURBANCE
Jerk resistance is not the constraint. Grip QUALITY is, and the monolithic
policy has 11-13 points more of it.
================================================================================

WHY THIS TEST
Every previous measurement of mid-carry drops ran the learned transport policy,
so grip quality and trajectory quality were confounded: "the policy shakes the
object loose" and "the grip was weak" predict the same data. grip_jerk_test.py
removes the transport policy. It grasps, certifies the lift with the deployed
criterion (8 held steps, 20-step scripted lift, >=3cm rise), then applies a
SCRIPTED open-loop motion under TRANSPORT's own rule layer (a[3:6]=0 rotation
discarded, a[6]=1.0 gripper forced closed). Whatever drops now is the grip.

Three profiles, so "jerk" is tested against a control rather than assumed:
  hold      zero translation                      -- control
  carry     constant 0.5 on one axis (=CARRY_CLIP)-- steady travel
  jerk      bang-bang +-0.5 every 3 steps         -- acceleration/deceleration
  jerkhard  bang-bang +-1.0 every 2 steps         -- max the controller allows

3 arms x 12 eval seeds x 100 episodes. Direction = ep%4 -> +x,-x,+y,-y, so all
arms see identical spawns AND identical disturbances; comparisons are paired.
10800 episodes in the main grid, 3600 in the jerkhard supplement.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 1: JERK IS NOT THE MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
survival is conditional on lift certification -- the population that reaches
TRANSPORT on the real pipeline.

  arm                     hold     carry    jerk    jerkhard   jerkhard-hold
  monolithic v7 FP32     98.14%   97.13%   98.14%   98.14%       +0.00
  decomposed FP32        95.59%   94.12%   95.67%   95.59%       +0.00
  decomposed INT8        87.26%   84.03%   87.17%   86.98%       -0.28

Doubling to maximum amplitude changes survival by -0.00/-0.09/-0.19 pts
(paired, p=nan/0.59/0.17, 0-2 of 12 seeds down). Nothing.

THE PROFILES DID MOVE THE ARM -- this is not a null test. Measured eef motion
during the disturbance window (median, 25 episodes, decomposed FP32):

  profile    path(m)   NET DISP(m)   v_max(m/s)   A_MAX(m/s^2)
  hold        0.0196     0.0151        0.074         0.80
  carry       0.3586     0.3163        0.140         1.44
  jerk        0.2135     0.0182        0.119         2.19
  jerkhard    0.3226     0.0283        0.207         4.04

jerkhard delivers 5x the control's acceleration and 2.8x carry's, and costs
nothing. carry has the LOWEST acceleration of the three moving profiles and the
LARGEST net displacement, and is the only one that costs anything (-1.0 to
-3.2 pts). The damaging variable is DISPLACEMENT, not acceleration.

Mechanically this is unsurprising in hindsight: 4.04 m/s^2 is 0.41g lateral,
and that is the most the OSC_POSE controller can deliver at full command. The
actuator cannot generate enough lateral acceleration to shake a certified grip
loose. Hardening the grip against jerk optimises against a disturbance this
robot cannot produce. What travel does instead is change the arm CONFIGURATION
-- which is the joint-limit story, not a grip story.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 2: THE MONOLITHIC GRIP IS 11-13 POINTS BETTER, AND IT IS BETTER TWICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  arm                     lift-cert   survives carry   BOTH
  monolithic v7 FP32        98.67%        98.14%      96.83%
  decomposed FP32           96.33%        95.59%      92.08%
  decomposed INT8           87.67%        87.17%      76.42%

Paired per-seed, all three profiles, 12/12 seeds up, 0 down:
  monolithic vs decomposed INT8   +10.93 / +13.11 / +11.02 pts   p < 0.0001
  decomposed FP32 vs INT8          +8.37 / +10.10 /  +8.54 pts   p < 0.0001

INT8 loses ~11 points at certification AND another ~11 at survival. It is weak
at both ends, not at one.

SUGGESTIVE, NOT PROOF: the BOTH column tracks measured end-to-end success
closely -- FP32 92.08% vs 90.67% actual, INT8 76.42% vs 78.33% actual. Grip
retention through a SCRIPTED carry nearly reproduces end-to-end performance,
i.e. the learned transport policy adds little on top of "did the grip survive
being moved". This is consistent with, and independently explains, transport
retraining having failed twice over six seeds. It is an agreement of two
numbers, not a controlled attribution -- the disturbance is not the real
trajectory, and RETRACT failures and jams are outside this harness.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 3 (NEGATIVE): A HANDOFF JOINT-MARGIN GATE WOULD FIRE ON NOISE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built to test "tune the FSM to reach a stable posture after grasping". It
inverts: pinned episodes survive BETTER.

  decomposed INT8, jerk    pinned <0.02  n=410  survive 89.76%
                           tight .02-.10 n=136  survive 92.65%
                           clear  >0.10  n=506  survive 83.60%

Cause: 395 of those 410 "pinned" cases are JOINT 5, whose lower bound is
-0.0175 ~ 0, so a normal reaching posture reads as pinned by construction. Same
trap documented in regrasp_jam_diag.py. A handoff-margin gate would fire on
39% of INT8 episodes, almost all benign. Do not build it.

THE JAM FINDING SURVIVES THIS. Re-checked against Results/rj_diag: jammed
episodes end at margin -0.0004 (actually violating the limit), successes at
+0.43 (INT8) / +0.58 (FP32). Being near joint 5's bound at handoff is common
and harmless; being stuck against it for 285 of 300 carry steps is the failure.
The two measurements are not the same variable.

Note without a conclusion attached: the monolithic arm is pinned at handoff
0.00% of 1184 times, decomposed 19.4% (FP32) / 39.0% (INT8). A structurally
different posture. On this evidence it is not what drives its advantage.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 4: CORNER GRIPS ARE AN INT8 PROBLEM SPECIFICALLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
survival under jerk by grip angle from a flat face:

  arm                      0-15 deg   15-30 deg   30-46 deg   spread
  monolithic v7 FP32        99.59%     97.21%      97.03%     -2.6
  decomposed FP32           96.80%     95.05%      94.74%     -2.1
  decomposed INT8           93.99%     82.63%      82.78%    -11.2

This REVISES Results/monolithic_control.txt, which concluded "corner grips were
never the problem". That holds for FP32 and monolithic. It is false for INT8,
where quantization turns a tolerable non-flat grip into a fragile one. The
earlier document tested lift certification only; this tests retention under
motion, which is where the angle actually bites.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE THREE PROPOSED DIRECTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. "Train a grip that survives jerk"        REFUTED as posed. Jerk resistance
   is not the binding variable; the controller cannot produce damaging
   acceleration. But the INTENT -- a better grip -- is correct and is the
   largest single lever measured in this campaign (+11-13 pts).
2. "FSM stabilisation after grasp"           The joint-margin form is dead
   (Result 3). Not yet tested: an unconditional repositioning move.
3. "Retrain on a wider dropzone"             Unchanged, still deferred. Note it
   only improves recovery AFTER a drop; a better grip reduces drop frequency,
   which dominates.
