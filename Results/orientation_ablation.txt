WHAT DOES PERCEPTION'S ORIENTATION HALF ACTUALLY DO?
====================================================
Of the 46 observation dims, perception supplies 14 (the rest is
proprioception the robot has without any camera):
    obj_pos           [0:3]    from the object's POSITION
    obj_quat          [3:7]    from its ORIENTATION
    obj_to_eef_pos    [7:10]   position, in the gripper frame
    obj_to_eef_quat  [10:14]   orientation, in the gripper frame

Ablation: replace the orientation dims with an identity quaternion and
re-run the full FSM. 6 seeds x 50 episodes, random spawn, --regrasp
--rc-steps 60, grasp c2m512_s1/best, place td3_place/best.

  configuration                              mean     paired vs baseline
  baseline (true orientation)               95.67%    --
  obj_quat -> identity                      94.67%    -1.00 pts,
                                                      95% CI [-6.59, +4.59]
  obj_quat AND obj_to_eef_quat -> identity  11.33%   -84.33 pts,
                                                      95% CI [-90.89, -77.78]

THE SPLIT IS SHARP. The object's orientation in the WORLD frame is worth
about a point -- indistinguishable from noise. Its orientation in the
GRIPPER frame is essential: remove it and the task collapses to 11%.

That reconciles two results that looked contradictory. The yaw diagnostic
(Results/yaw_alignment.txt) found the policy does NOT align jaws to the
object -- alignment error is uniform, mean 20.9 deg against 22.5 for
ignoring yaw entirely. So the policy does not use orientation as a target
to null out. But obj_to_eef_quat is a RELATIVE quantity, and the policy
leans on it as a servoing signal: how am I oriented with respect to the
thing. Not aligning is not the same as not looking.

It also explains the 28% collapse under a flipped pose (apriltag_sim.py,
the reason the upright prior exists): a flipped pose corrupts the RELATIVE
block, not only the absolute one.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR PERCEPTION
  - Position alone is NOT sufficient. The two-fold ambiguity
    disambiguation and the upright prior stay justified, now on a
    measurement against a healthy baseline rather than an anecdote.
  - min_up's rejections cannot be relaxed to raise the detection rate.
  - But absolute world orientation is nearly free, so what perception must
    get right is orientation RELATIVE to the gripper -- a weaker
    requirement than full 6-DOF pose for a near-cubic object.
  - A toppled tag stays a real failure: losing the detection loses the
    relative block too.

METHOD WARNING -- THIS ABLATION WAS RUN THREE TIMES
The first two had broken baselines and gave the OPPOSITE conclusion for
obj_quat:
    baseline 57.67%  (missing --regrasp --rc-steps 60)  obj_quat -12.00 pts
    baseline 59.00%  (--place-ckpt pointed at the parent directory, so
                      load_actor silently took td3_place/actor_td3 instead
                      of td3_place/best/actor_td3)      obj_quat -11.33 pts
    baseline 95.67%  (correct)                          obj_quat  -1.00 pts
A degraded control has no slack, so every perturbation looks catastrophic
and an ablation reads as significant when it is not. An ablation is only
interpretable against a control that matches the deployed system, and
fsm_sim accepts a checkpoint directory that LOOKS valid while quietly
loading a weaker policy.
