================================================================================
TRANSPORT RETRAINING: TWO NEGATIVE RESULTS
The original fixed-spawn transport policy remains the best artifact.
================================================================================

MOTIVATION
After lift-certifying the grasp stage, end-to-end reached 82% with the ORIGINAL
transport policy -- trained long ago against a fixed spawn and the old grasp
model. Remaining failures were transport_stall (9) and timeout_dropped (5), so
retraining transport against the new grasp distribution looked like the obvious
next lever. It was not.

SETUP
train_place.py gained a CLI (--grasp-chkpt-dir, --random-spawn,
--place-chkpt-dir, --warm-start-from, --critic-reset-every, --layer-norm); the
grasp checkpoint and fixed spawn had both been hardcoded. All runs: 8000
episodes, 5 envs, native random spawn (position + rotation), grasp stage =
liftcert_phaseB_s2/best, warm-started from td3_place/best, critics fresh.
Reset arms use LayerNorm critics with head resets every 7000 updates (~7 per
run, matched to the grasp ablation's cadence -- see the note below).

END-TO-END LADDER (test_place.py, 100 episodes, --random-spawn, same grasp)
  policy                                    success
  ORIGINAL transport (never retrained)        82%
  retrained control  s0 / s1 / s2        75 / 38 / 52   mean 55.0  sd 18.5
  retrained + resets s0 / s1 / s2        16 / 58 /  1   mean 25.0  sd 30.5

RESULT 1 -- retraining transport on the new distribution HURT.
The best retrained seed (75%) is below the untouched original (82%). A policy
trained directly on rotated holds generalises worse than one that never saw
them. Not investigated further; the original is kept.

RESULT 2 -- critic resets HURT the place stage, badly.
Mean 55 -> 25, with seed 2 collapsing to 1/100 (transport_stall:82). This is
the OPPOSITE sign to the grasp stage, where the same remedy was worth +20.3
(level 1.0) and +30.0 (level 2.0), 3/3 seeds each.

WHY THE SIGN FLIPS (hypothesis, not established)
Transport does ~6.7 gradient steps per episode against grasp's ~48. After each
reset the critic has far fewer updates to recover before the next one, so the
run spends most of its time in the post-reset hole rather than the
post-recovery peak. The place stage also runs a carry curriculum, and a wrecked
critic stalls curriculum advancement -- the grasp task has no analogue.

The takeaway for the thesis is that plasticity-loss resets are NOT a general
fix for this pipeline. They are specific to the grasp stage's learning
dynamics, and applying them by analogy to another stage cost 30 points.

TWO MEASUREMENT TRAPS HIT ALONG THE WAY
1. Reset interval was first set to 20000 updates, copied from the grasp stage
   without rederiving it. That fired only 1 reset by episode 3000 (~2-3 over a
   full run) instead of ~7. The arms were relaunched at 7000. Sizing a
   schedule in gradient steps does not transfer between stages with different
   episode lengths.
2. best_metric is frac50 x place50, so it mixes curriculum progress with
   placement skill. Comparing arms at DIFFERENT episode counts made controls
   look better purely because they had advanced further through the curriculum.
   Only matched-episode or end-to-end numbers are meaningful.

Also fixed: test_place.py loaded the place policy as a full td3.Agent, pulling
critics it never uses, which crashed on every LayerNorm checkpoint. It now
loads the actor alone, as place_env_wrapper.py already does for the grasp
stage.

STATUS
Pipeline best remains: lift-certified grasp (liftcert_phaseB_s2) + ORIGINAL
transport = 82% end-to-end under full position and rotation randomisation,
against 92% for the fixed-pose baseline in thesis_context Sec 8.4.
