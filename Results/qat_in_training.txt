================================================================================
QAT INSIDE THE RL LOOP: +4.43 INT8 POINTS, AND IT OVERTURNS AN EARLIER VERDICT
The quantisation gap falls 5.63 -> 1.89. Quantisation is no longer the binding
constraint; FP32 capacity is.
================================================================================

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY REFUTED BEFORE
Results/int8_deployment.txt concluded "QAT WAS THE DAMAGE" -- 50-epoch QAT cost
12.7 points and gripfix_s2 lost 36.2. That verdict is correct about what was
tested and WRONG as a verdict on QAT. qat_finetune.py:184 optimised
    loss = F.mse_loss(qat_out, teacher_out)
i.e. output fidelity to an FP32 teacher on a frozen calibration buffer. The
same document's Finding 2 measured that objective as ANTI-correlated with
deployed success (best corr 0.952 -> worst behaviour 37.1%; worst corr 0.872 ->
best behaviour 70.2%). Finding 3's "600 epochs moved MSE 0.275990 -> 0.276077,
that is a floor" is a statement about THAT loss, not about quantisation damage
being irrecoverable.

WHAT IS TESTED HERE
td3.Agent.enable_actor_fakequant: per-tensor symmetric INT8 fake-quant on the
actor's Linear weights with a straight-through estimator, inside the RL loop,
so the RETURN is optimised under the quantisation that actually ships. The
quantised actor also drives choose_action, so the replay data reflects the
quantised behaviour policy. Target networks stay FP32 -- they are a
training-time bootstrap and quantising them only adds target noise.

Verified before launch: forward changes under fake-quant; weights stay FP32
(quantisation is in the forward, not storage); all 255 int8 levels used; STE
gradients update weights; residual quantise-dequantise error 0.00036 against
mean|w| 0.081.

DESIGN
Both arms carry --actor-wclip 8 and share warm start, config and training
seeds, so QAT is the SINGLE variable. 6 training seeds x 12 eval seeds x 100
episodes per cell. Arms share both levels, so pairing is on (training seed,
eval seed) = 72 pairs. All 9 new artifacts converted with --per_tensor and
verified to carry exactly 1 scale per quantised tensor (8088 bytes, identical
to the deployed artifact).

THE GAP IS NEARLY CLOSED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  per-tensor INT8 quantisation gap, this session
    no clip        (wclip0)          11.22
    clip k=8       (3 seeds)          5.00
    clip k=8       (6 seeds)          5.63
    clip k=8 + QAT (6 seeds)          1.89
    transport model (quantises free)  0.00
Two independent interventions took the gap from 11.22 to 1.89. Note they act on
different things: clipping caps max/std, and Results/weight_range_regularisation
showed that mechanism EXHAUSTED (k=4 reached max/std 4.00, below transport's
free-quantising 5.7, and still left 4.72). QAT closes what remains, which
confirms the residue was never dynamic range.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE 90% TARGET
At a 1.89-point gap, INT8 >= 90% needs FP32 >= 91.89%. Best FP32 observed
across ~21 cells this session is 90.67%. So quantisation is no longer the
binding constraint -- the decomposed 5.2k actor's FP32 ceiling is. That
reverses the situation at the start of the session, when the gap was 12.3 and
capacity was second-order.

CAVEATS
- Control at 6 seeds (74.99%) reads BELOW deployed bi_s0 (78.33%); seeds 3-5
  trained worse. So QAT's mean is +1.09 over the deployed artifact, while the
  matched causal effect over its own control is +4.43. Do not quote the first
  as the effect or the second as a deployment delta.
- Best single QAT cell is 84.83%, best-of-6, i.e. a selected maximum. The
  wclip campaign showed such a maximum halving on held-out seeds. Not confirmed.
- best_metric during training also favoured QAT (0.9125 vs 0.8833, 6/6 seeds),
  which is consistent but is a biased max-over-window statistic and grasp-stage
  only. It is corroboration, not evidence.

NEXT
Capacity is now the binding constraint, and Results/monolithic_control.txt
already names the teacher: v7 at 2.2M params certifies 98.7% of grasps against
the decomposed stage's 79.2%. A 256/128 actor (45,440 weights, ~45KB INT8) fits
the ESP32 budget (4MB flash, 320KB SRAM, 40KB arena, 9.5ms of 50ms), but it
cannot inherit the 5-stage warm-start curriculum -- shape mismatch -- so it
needs either the ladder re-run at the new width or function-preserving widening
(Net2WiderNet) from the trained 64/32 policy. That is the next real experiment.
