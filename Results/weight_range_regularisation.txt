================================================================================
WEIGHT-RANGE REGULARISATION FOR PER-TENSOR INT8
Capping max/std recovers ~7 points of INT8 success at no FP32 cost, and
removes the catastrophic-collapse failure mode.
================================================================================

THE MECHANISM BEING TESTED
Per-tensor INT8 sets ONE scale per tensor from the largest weight, so a
tensor's max/std IS its quantisation cost (Results/int8_deployment.txt Fnd 4):
transport quantises FREE at 5.7, gripfix_s2 costs -7.2 at 9.3, bi_s0 -14.0 at
10.0. td3.Agent._clip_actor_weights projects every actor Linear weight onto
|w| <= k*std(w) after each actor update. Verified to hit the fixed point
exactly: 9.96/10.82 -> 6.00/6.00 at k=6; tensors already below k untouched.

DESIGN
4 arms (k = 0/4/6/8) x 3 training seeds x {FP32, INT8} x 12 eval seeds x 100
episodes = 28800 episodes. Training config is bi_s0's manifest verbatim, k the
only variable; k=0 is a FRESH control at matched seeds, not the old run.
Arms share training seeds AND eval seeds, so (training seed, eval seed) is a
legitimate pairing unit -- 36 pairs per comparison.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT: k=6 AND k=8 WIN, k=4 OVER-CONSTRAINS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  arm     max/std     FP32%          INT8%         gap        NET INT8 vs ctrl
  wclip0  8.3-10.3    83.39 +-8.15   72.17 +-10.84  11.22 +-14.85     --
  wclip4  4.00        76.39 +-13.72  71.67 +-8.55    4.72 +-5.72    -0.50  ns
  wclip6  6.00        83.67 +-5.06   79.42 +-2.20    4.25 +-4.70    +7.25  p=0.0002
  wclip8  ~8.00       83.81 +-6.53   78.81 +-6.59    5.00 +-0.68    +6.64  p=0.0035

  paired, 36 pairs:  wclip6 27/9 up, wclip8 24/12 up
  FP32 cost:         wclip6 +0.28 (p=0.87), wclip8 +0.42 (p=0.64) -- free
                     wclip4 -7.00 (p=0.019) -- the constraint bites the policy

All three clipped arms cut the gap from 11.22 to 4.25-5.00, exactly as Finding
4 predicts. k=4 closes the gap too but pays 7 FP32 points for it, so the net is
zero: the trade turns over between k=4 and k=6.

SECOND EFFECT, ARGUABLY THE MORE IMPORTANT ONE: IT REMOVES THE COLLAPSE
Per-tensor quantisation does not degrade a policy smoothly -- it occasionally
destroys one. Control seed 2: FP32 84.92% -> INT8 59.67%, a 25-point collapse.
The same seed under k=6: 77.83% -> 78.58%, no loss at all. Across seeds,
INT8 sd falls from 10.84 (control) to 2.20 (k=6); gap sd from 14.85 to 0.68
(k=8). Clipping buys predictability as much as it buys points, which matters
more for a deployed artifact than the mean does.

HEAD-TO-HEAD vs THE DEPLOYED ARTIFACT (12 eval seeds x 100, A+C rule layer)
  wclip8_s0   84.67%  vs  78.33%   +6.33  t=4.05 p=0.0019  10/2 seeds up
  wclip6_s0   81.92%  vs  78.33%   +3.58  t=2.13 p=0.0570   8/3
  wclip8_s2   80.08%  vs  78.33%   +1.75  ns
  wclip6_s1   77.75%  vs  78.33%   -0.58  ns
SELECTION CAVEAT: wclip8_s0 is the best of 12 cells, so +6.33 is a selected
maximum, not an unbiased estimate. The unbiased causal effect of clipping is
the arm-level +7.25 / +6.64 against the matched control.

HELD-OUT CONFIRMATION -- THE CANDIDATE CLAIM SHRINKS BY HALF AND LOSES p<0.05
12 eval seeds disjoint from the sweep (13 59 71 137 199 257 331 419 547 601
733 911), 100 episodes each. The deployed model was RE-MEASURED on the same
fresh seeds rather than compared against its known 78.33%, so a shift in seed
difficulty cannot masquerade as a treatment effect -- and there was one:
deployed INT8 reads 80.42% here against 78.33% on the sweep seeds.

  model      FP32%    INT8%    gap
  deployed   89.67%   80.42%  +9.25
  wclip8s0   90.50%   83.33%  +7.17
  wclip6s0   88.50%   81.83%  +6.67

  paired vs deployed, 12 pairs
    INT8  wclip8s0  +2.92  p=0.0787  8/3 up     (was +6.33 p=0.0019 selected)
          wclip6s0  +1.42  p=0.2312  6/4 up     (was +3.58 p=0.0570)
    FP32  wclip8s0  +0.83  p=0.3177  7/5        (no FP32 cost after all)
          wclip6s0  -1.17  p=0.2497  5/7

READ THIS CAREFULLY, THE TWO CLAIMS HAVE DIFFERENT STANDING:
  1. CLIPPING WORKS. The arm-level +7.25 (p=0.0002, 36 pairs, k=6) compares
     matched arms across all 3 training seeds and involved no selection. It
     stands unchanged. The gap reduction reproduces here too: 9.25 deployed
     vs 7.17 / 6.67 clipped.
  2. wclip8_s0 AS A SPECIFIC ARTIFACT IS NOT CONFIRMED. +2.92 at p=0.0787 does
     not clear 0.05. Direction is consistent (8/3 seeds up) and n=12 single-cell
     pairs is far less powerful than the 36-pair arm test, so this is an
     underpowered test of a shrunken estimate, NOT evidence of no effect. The
     honest point estimate for this artifact is +2.92, not +6.33.

One earlier worry is retired: wclip8_s0's FP32 was 89.08% on the selection
seeds, below the deployed 90.67%, suggesting the clip cost FP32 quality. On
held-out seeds it reads 90.50% vs deployed 89.67% (+0.83, ns). The apparent
FP32 cost was seed noise.

DO NOT ship wclip8_s0 on the strength of the sweep maximum. The supported route
is to retrain k=6/k=8 over more seeds and select on a held-out criterion, or
accept the arm-level effect and pick any k>=6 model by median rather than max.

TRAINING-SEED VARIANCE DOMINATES RAW PERFORMANCE
Control FP32 across three seeds: 74.58 / 84.92 / 90.67 -- a 16-point spread.
Any single-seed reading of this sweep would have been noise, and the control
arm's mean (72.17% INT8) sits BELOW the deployed bi_s0 (78.33%) purely because
two of its three seeds trained badly. Clipping improves the FP32->INT8
TRANSFER; it does not rescue a bad FP32 policy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A METHOD ERROR WORTH RECORDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first pass of this experiment (288 shards, 28800 episodes) was run with
conversions that DEFAULTED TO PER-CHANNEL, because --skip_qat was passed
without --per_tensor. It measured nothing: per-channel gives each output
channel its own scale, so a single outlier weight cannot poison a tensor and
weight clipping has nothing to do. The control gap there was 2.36 points
against 11.22 here, and no arm separated.

The tell was present and ignored: the artifacts were 10488 bytes against the
deployed 8088, noticed and dismissed as "converter metadata" BEFORE the shards
were launched. Byte-size mismatch against a known-good artifact is a
first-class signal, not a footnote.

The per-channel run also appeared to show +9 points for free over the deployed
model. That is NOT deployable: qat_and_convert.py:233 records that ESP-NN's
per-channel int8 kernels return mid-range outputs "badly wrong, even
sign-flipped" on device. Those numbers came from TFLite host reference kernels
the ESP32 never runs -- precisely the host-vs-artifact gap Sec 9.11 exists to
close.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATUS AND NEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Adopt-able: --actor-wclip 6 (or 8) as a default for any actor destined for
per-tensor INT8. It is one projection after the optimiser step, costs nothing
in FP32, and both removes ~7 points of quantisation loss and the collapse mode.
Done: held-out confirmation (above) -- effect real at arm level, candidate
artifact not individually confirmed.
Not yet done: retrain k=8 over more seeds and select by held-out median;
re-run the grip-jerk
harness on a clipped model to see whether the +7 lands on lift certification,
on retention under motion, or both; mirror nothing to the .ino yet.
