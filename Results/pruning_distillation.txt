PRUNING AND DISTILLATION OF THE GRASP ACTOR
===========================================
Teacher: checkpoints/td3_grasp_rand_td3_ln_c2m512_s1/best, 64x32, 5,319
params. Task success measured through the full FSM, --regrasp --rc-steps 60,
random spawn, bread, 3 seeds x 50 episodes per cell (teacher: 6 seeds).

SCOPE FIRST, SO THE NUMBERS ARE NOT OVERSOLD
The grasp actor is 5.2 KB as INT8. The AprilTag detector needs 177 KB of
RAM. Compressing the policy CANNOT relieve the memory pressure this project
actually has -- it could not even fund the ~22 KB that restoring
bits_corrected=1 would need, because the actor lives in flash and the
detector's cost is heap. This is a compression study, not a deployment
optimisation.

STRUCTURED, NOT MASKED
Pruning here removes whole hidden units and produces a smaller dense
network. Unstructured magnitude masking saves nothing on this target:
TFLite Micro has no sparse kernels, so the zeros still occupy the tensor.

PRUNING ALONE DOES NOT SURVIVE
Mean |action error| against the teacher on a [-1,1] action range,
immediately after removing units, with no retraining:
    48x24  0.5090     24x12  0.6288
    32x16  0.6388     16x8   0.6276     12x6  0.7782
Recovery training is not optional; it is the method.

DISTILLATION (60,000 teacher-visited states, 60 epochs)
States come from teacher rollouts through the deployed FSM, not from random
sampling: the visited manifold is a vanishing fraction of a 46-D space.

  size    params   INT8    action err          task success
                           pruned  random    pruned    random
  64x32    5,319  5.2 KB     --      --      95.67%      --    (teacher)
  48x24    3,607  3.5 KB   0.0685  0.1255    90.67%    80.00%
  32x16    2,151  2.1 KB   0.1262  0.1487    83.33%    57.33%
  24x12    1,519  1.5 KB   0.1506  0.1724    66.00%    16.67%
  16x8       951  0.9 KB   0.2194  0.1998     4.00%     2.00%

PRUNED INITIALISATION IS WORTH A LOT, AND MORE THAN FIDELITY SUGGESTS
At every size above the smallest, starting from the pruned teacher beats
random init. The gap in ACTION ERROR looks modest (0.126 vs 0.149 at
32x16); the gap in TASK SUCCESS is 26 points. At 24x12 it is 49 points
against an action-error gap of 0.02.

That divergence is the most useful thing here. Mean action error is a poor
proxy for task success: a small average error concentrated at the moments
that matter -- the approach and the closure -- costs far more than a larger
error spread over the trajectory. Compression work that selects on
fidelity alone will pick the wrong model.

At 16x8 the ordering reverses (random 0.1998 beats pruned 0.2194) and both
collapse to ~3%. Once 82% of the units are gone the inherited structure is
a constraint rather than a head start, and neither network has the capacity
for the task at all.

WHERE THE CLIFF IS
  48x24 (68% of params) costs 5 points -- a reasonable trade.
  32x16 (40%) costs 12.
  24x12 (29%) costs 30.
  16x8  (18%) is not a policy.
So roughly a third of the parameters can go for ~12 points, and below a
quarter the task is lost. The teacher was already small; there was not much
redundancy to find.

WHAT THIS IS ACTUALLY USEFUL FOR
Not saving RAM. The per-object deployment (Results/object_generalisation.txt
-- one policy per object, selected by AprilTag id) needs FOUR actors instead
of one. At 5.2 KB each that is ~21 KB of flash; at 48x24 distilled it is
~14 KB for about 5 points per object. That is the trade this study informs.
