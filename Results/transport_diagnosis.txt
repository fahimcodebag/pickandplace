WHY CEREAL FAILS AFTER A GOOD GRASP
===================================
Prompted by watching 15 rendered episodes (Results/cereal_video). Grasp
policy alignwarm_s0 (grasp metric 1.000, task 64%), place policy = bread's.

WHAT THE FAILURES ARE
  15 episodes: 11 OK, 4 FAIL. All 4 failures:
    phase DONE_FAIL, regrasps used 0, steps 300/300/300/197
  Instrumented over 10 more episodes:
    failures  grasped_frac 1.00  min_xy_to_bin 0.196 / 0.241 / 0.433
    successes grasped_frac 1.00  min_xy_to_bin 0.151 - 0.175
    threshold NEAR_TARGET_XY = 0.18
The grip never fails. The arm holds the box for the full 300-step horizon
and never closes the last few centimetres to the bin, so TRANSPORT never
hands over to RECENTER and the descent never runs. The whole distribution
straddles the 0.18 m threshold.

TRANSPORT IS DRIVEN BY THE PLACE ACTOR, NOT THE P CONTROLLER
p_xy_to_bin only runs in RECENTER and DESCEND, which these episodes never
reach. The carry is the learned place policy -- trained on BREAD.

CARRY HEIGHT IS DECISIVE, AND HIGHER IS BETTER
Replacing the place actor with P control plus a height target, cereal,
2-3 eval seeds x 50 episodes:
    place actor (bread-trained)   64.0%
    scripted P, carry_z 0.95      29.3%
    scripted P, carry_z 1.10      55.0%
    scripted P, carry_z 1.30      66.0%
So the objects being carried to 1.1-2.0 m for a bin at 0.80 is NOT waste.
Carrying low fails badly. At a high enough carry height simple P control
matches the learned place actor (66.0 vs 64.0, n=2 vs n=3 -- a tie, not a
win). The place actor is doing real work, and the open-loop TEST_LIFT that
produces the height is serving a purpose nobody documented.

The obstacle was NOT identified: only bin leg geoms are named in the model,
so the wall height could not be measured. Candidates are bin walls or
dividers, or better arm manipulability higher in the workspace. The height
curve is measured; the explanation is a hypothesis.

A SEPARATE LATENT BUG: TOUCHDOWN CAN NEVER FIRE
  touched = s[OBJ_Z] <= BIN_Z + TOUCH_MARGIN     # 0.80 + 0.02 = 0.82
OBJ_Z is the object's CENTRE, and a resting object's centre sits at the bin
floor plus its half-height: bread 0.8248, cereal 0.875. Neither can reach
0.82 without being pushed through the floor. DESCEND therefore always exits
on `stalled` or the 30-step cap, never on detecting touchdown -- for every
object, including bread. Same bug class as _GRIP_RANGE and BIN_X/BIN_Y:
a constant fixed to one object's geometry.

REFUTED ALONG THE WAY
  - "The 12-step RETRACT window scores late-settling objects as failures."
    Tested by simulating 120 extra steps after the FSM gave up: NONE of the
    failures became successful. The objects genuinely never reach the bin.
  - "The place actor is the bottleneck, so replace it with P control."
    At my first carry height (0.95) that HALVED success, 64% -> 29%.
